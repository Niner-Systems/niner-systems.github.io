## Headsets we've tested

We test our adapters against a wide variety of GA headsets &mdash; old and new, ANR and passive. Pete uses his **Bose A20** and **Lightspeed Delta Zulu** with the USB2GA daily, and we've also tested with **Lightspeed Zulu 3** headsets and a set of **FlightCom** headsets from the late 1990s. Whether your headset has ANR or not, the adapter is safe and compatible.

## Speakers

Just like in the plane, you may need to adjust the volume depending on the headset. Speaker impedance can differ from one manufacturer or model to another, though we've found many GA headsets are around 300 ohms. The audio chip in the adapter handles a wide range, which gives us confidence that any modern general aviation headset will work properly.

## Microphones

Microphones vary more between manufacturers. Some are 150 ohms, others 30 or 300, and we've even come across 2,200-ohm mics, so we focus our testing on microphones and buy and test as many variations as we can. The adapter supplies **+12V phantom power (mic bias)** from the USB port; general aviation mics tend to accept 8&ndash;16V, so they should all be compatible. ([Why aviation mics need this power](/faq/aviation-headset-mic-not-working-on-computer/).)

## Military headsets

We've heard military headsets have much lower impedances &mdash; roughly 19 ohms for the speakers and 5 ohms for the mics. That can cause a significant increase in current flow ([Ohm's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm's_law)), and we have not tested them. We're open to testing in a controlled environment if we can get our hands on some.
